# Rice Fertilization Guide - Best Practices for Bangladesh

Rice is the staple crop of Bangladesh, and proper fertilization is critical for maximizing yield and quality. This guide covers the complete fertilization program for rice cultivation in Bangladesh.

## Growth Stages and Fertilizer Requirements

### Stage 1: Land Preparation (Before Transplanting)

**Timing**: 7-10 days before transplanting

**Fertilizer Application**:
- **TSP (Triple Super Phosphate)**: 35-45 kg/acre
- **MOP (Muriate of Potash)**: 25-30 kg/acre

**Why**: Phosphorus and potassium should be applied as basal dose as they are less mobile in soil.

### Stage 2: Tillering (15-20 Days After Transplanting)

**Timing**: 15-20 DAT (Days After Transplanting)

**Fertilizer Application**:
- **Urea**: 40-50 kg/acre

**Why**: Nitrogen promotes vegetative growth and tiller formation during this critical stage.

### Stage 3: Panicle Initiation (35-40 DAT)

**Timing**: 35-40 DAT

**Fertilizer Application**:
- **Urea**: 30-40 kg/acre

**Why**: Second dose of nitrogen supports panicle development and grain formation.

### Stage 4: Flowering (55-60 DAT)

**Timing**: 55-60 DAT

**Fertilizer Application**:
- **Urea**: 20-30 kg/acre

**Why**: Final nitrogen application ensures proper grain filling and quality.

## Total Fertilizer Requirements per Acre

| Fertilizer | Total Quantity | Application Method |
|------------|---------------|-------------------|
| Urea | 100-120 kg | Split into 3 applications |
| TSP | 35-45 kg | Single basal application |
| MOP | 25-30 kg | Single basal application |
| Gypsum | 20-25 kg | Basal (if soil is deficient in sulfur) |

## Seasonal Considerations

### Boro Rice (Winter Season)
- Requires more nitrogen due to longer growing period
- Apply zinc sulfate if deficiency symptoms appear
- Increase potash by 10-15% for better cold tolerance

### Aman Rice (Monsoon Season)
- Reduce nitrogen application slightly due to higher organic matter mineralization
- Ensure proper drainage to prevent nutrient loss through leaching
- Monitor for iron deficiency in waterlogged conditions

### Aus Rice (Summer Season)
- Apply 10-15% more potassium for drought tolerance
- Split urea application into smaller doses to prevent losses
- Consider additional magnesium if soil is deficient

## Application Best Practices

### Method of Application

1. **Broadcasting**: Most common method for small fields
2. **Deep Placement**: More efficient, reduces nitrogen loss by 30-40%
3. **Foliar Application**: Useful for micronutrients during critical stages

### Timing Guidelines

- Apply fertilizers in the morning or late afternoon
- Avoid application during heavy rain
- Ensure 2-3 cm water in field when applying urea
- Drain water 24 hours before and after other fertilizer application

## Soil Testing Recommendations

For optimal results, conduct soil testing before the season:

- **pH**: Optimal range 6.0-7.0 for rice
- **Organic Matter**: Should be 2-3% for good fertility
- **Available Phosphorus**: Minimum 10 ppm
- **Exchangeable Potassium**: Minimum 0.15 meq/100g

## Common Problems and Solutions

### Yellowing of Leaves
**Cause**: Nitrogen deficiency
**Solution**: Apply 20-25 kg urea per acre immediately

### Purple Discoloration
**Cause**: Phosphorus deficiency
**Solution**: Apply phosphorus through foliar spray (2% DAP solution)

### Poor Tillering
**Cause**: Insufficient nitrogen or poor plant spacing
**Solution**: Apply additional nitrogen and ensure proper spacing (20cm x 15cm)
